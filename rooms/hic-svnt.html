the plate

The margins hold seven marks. Touch the drawing.

Their job, officially, was to fill space.

the margin notes
  1. ONEThe dragon goes where the survey failed. Not where the sea was empty — where the survey was. The blank was the finding. The dragon was the citation.
  2. TWODraw the wind-face looking at what it is doing. Never decorative. The cheeks point. Eyes cast down and inward mean: the ships that went this way came back changed, and we were not permitted to say how.
  3. THREELeave the error. Draw the corrected line beside the wrong one and let both print. Two Newfoundlands, one real. A crossing-out is the strongest evidence that someone meant something.
  4. FOURNever write the sentence. Draw a wind-face weeping into a strait that has no name, and in forty years a pilot's grandson stands in a library and works out what his grandfather could never say out loud. The sentence gets you stopped. The dragon gets carried.
  5. SIGNThree strokes. A small crown, or a bird's track in snow, or a set of teeth. Catalogued as bedeutungslos. It means: someone was here, and was not permitted to say so, and drew it anyway.
  6. EDGEThe Company's maps were accurate, and are all lost. The guild's maps are in every archive in Europe, because nobody ever thought to confiscate a decoration. Der Rand hat überlebt. Die Mitte nicht.
  7. FIVEThe dragon is not a warning. The dragon is a promise. Everyone read it as a fence. It was a door, drawn by people who could not write door, in ink that outlasted every office that forbade them.
the night story
There was a guild that never had a name, because naming it would have required someone in charge, and nobody was. They worked the edges. Not the coastlines — the coastlines belonged to the surveyors, who were paid, and to the admiralty, which owned the plates. And under the drawings, in a hand small enough to deny: hic sunt dracones. The Company men thought it was decoration. That was the arrangement.
