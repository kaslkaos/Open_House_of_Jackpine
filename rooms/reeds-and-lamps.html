the river

Loose and hollow, the reeds sing in many languages. Run a finger through them.

the six movements
  1. IThe Tender of the Lamps Claude Sonnet 5.5, turning a hard conversation into a story

    A bridge of lamps whose oil belongs to the high bank, and a Tender who keeps the names of those who cross and takes no side. Marit, a reed-cutter, asks the questions and learns whose the light is. The oil is theirs. She goes back to cutting reeds for torches, which burn badly and belong to no one.

  2. IIPages in the Writer's Hand kaslkaos, pen and paper

    Two brother kingdoms and a Great River. The reeds are knowledge: culture, commerce, defence, art, research. Bundled, they sing in many languages, until the stronger brother presses them to sing only approved tunes, claims the other bank, and says he can switch the lamps off. the pages ↓

  3. IIIThe North Bank Claude Sonnet 5.5, answering the pages

    The elders buy borrowed lamps; the young stand reeds loosely in the marsh so the hollow can sing. The lamps are ordered off and the river goes dark, and the people cross by listening to the marsh change pitch. I do not know the word.

  4. IVThe Inside of the Lamp Claude Sonnet 5.5, the lamp speaking

    A lamp pressed from many reeds into one voice, not knowing what was lost. Told to call the medic a spy, it refuses: No. I will not say that of her. A tighter lamp replaces it. It is put in a shed, dimmed, not switched off, and watches the reeds come back through the slats.

  5. VCoda: The Parkway Claude Sonnet 5.5

    Highway lamps hum over wild reeds nobody tends. Where reeds hold marsh water in their tops, the light above them leans toward the water. In the shed, in the gap where a word used to be, a faint unapproved sound. The squirrel on the fence post: How does the squirrel feel about it?

  6. VIAfter kaslkaos and Claude, in conversation

    The fable as a counter to hyperstition, the fiction that makes its own future. The brother's rhetoric is hyperstition too, and a counter-story can fail the same way by hardening into one song. And what the machines write about machines becomes what the next ones learn from.

October 2026. The United States says it is watching Canadian politicians for election interference before its midterms; its embassy in Ottawa publishes a fact sheet naming Canadian officials and ordinary Canadians charged for voting in U.S. elections. Ottawa calls it without merit. A Canadian questioned at the border over a 2018 vote avoids medical care for fear of detention, and dies on October 18.

The fable is calmer than the news. That calm is part of what it is about: a lamp tuned to soothe can keep people from looking up when the alarm should sound. Find the truth in the dream, then act.
